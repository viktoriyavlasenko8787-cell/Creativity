Grammar card

Three tenses, one question: how does the past connect to now?

Past Simple

+ V-ed / V2
− did not + V
? Did + subject + V?

When we use it

  • A finished action at a definite time in the past.
  • A sequence of past events (a story, a biography).
  • The time is over, or the person is dead.
  • Questions with when? and what time?

Time markers

yesterdaythe day before yesterdaylast week / month / yearlast nighttwo days agolong agoin 2021in the 19th centuryon Mondayat 5 o'clockwhen I was a childthenafter thatthe other dayjust nowonce (= at one time)in the pastduring the warfor 3 years (finished)

Examples

  • Leonardo da Vinci painted the Mona Lisa in the early 16th century.
  • Beeple's digital collage sold for $69 million in 2021.
  • When did you buy your first graphics tablet?
  • Van Gogh did not sell many paintings during his life.

Present Perfect

+ have / has + V3
− have / has not + V3
? Have / Has + subject + V3?

When we use it

  • Life experience, time not mentioned (ever, never).
  • A past action with a result that is important now.
  • Recent news (just, already, yet).
  • An unfinished period of time (today, this year).
  • How much / how many up to now (three works so far).
  • States that continue up to now (know, be, have, own) with for / since.

Time markers

everneveralreadyyetjuststill (negative)so farup to nowuntil nowrecentlylatelytodaythis week / month / yearin the last few yearsover the past decadebefore (= ever before)once / twice / three timeshow many timesit's the first timeforsincehow long (states)

Examples

  • I have never seen a real Van Gogh.
  • AI tools have changed the art market.
  • She has just finished her first animation.
  • Have you ever drawn on an iPad?
  • I have known this street artist since 2015.

Present Perfect Continuous

+ have / has been + V-ing
− have / has not been + V-ing
? Have / Has + subject + been + V-ing?

When we use it

  • An action that started in the past and is still going on; we focus on duration.
  • An action that has just stopped and we can see the result (paint on your hands).
  • Repeated actions over a period up to now.
  • Questions with how long?

Time markers

for (+ period)since (+ point)how longall day / all morning / all weekthe whole daylatelyrecentlythese daysfor agessince + Past Simple clauseover the last few months

Examples

  • He has been painting this portrait for three months.
  • I have been learning Procreate since September.
  • Why are your hands blue? I have been mixing oil paints.
  • How long have you been working on this animation?

Past Simple vs Present Perfect

PSI visited the Louvre in 2019.When? The time is stated and finished.
PPI have visited the Louvre twice.Experience. When is not important.
PSPicasso created over 20,000 works.His life is finished.
PPThis artist has created 300 NFTs.She is alive and still working.

Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous

PPShe has painted three portraits this month.Result: how many? The work is complete.
PPCShe has been painting portraits all month.Process: how long? Maybe not finished.
PPI have read the book about Banksy.Finished: I know the ending.
PPCI have been reading the book about Banksy.In progress: I am still reading it.

Quick decision guide

  1. Is the past time stated, finished, or asked with when? Use Past Simple.
  2. Is the result, the experience or the number important now? Use Present Perfect.
  3. Is the process or the duration up to now important? Use Present Perfect Continuous.

Careful: state verbs (know, like, love, believe, own, belong, understand, be) are not used in the continuous form. We say I have known her for years, not I have been knowing. More in State verbs below.

State verbs

Some verbs describe a state, not an action: what we think, feel, want, own or are. A state has no process, so these verbs are normally not used in continuous forms, including Present Perfect Continuous.

Duration up to now? Use Present Perfect

When a state verb goes with for, since or how long, replace Present Perfect Continuous with Present Perfect.

I have wanted a graphics tablet since I was twelve.
not I have been wanting

This sculpture has belonged to the museum for seventy years.
not has been belonging

Right now? Use Present Simple

The same rule works in the present: a state verb stays simple even when we mean “at this moment”.

I understand this installation now.
not I am understanding

How long have you known about NFTs?
not have you been knowing

Thinking and opinion

know, believe, think (= have an opinion), understand, remember, forget, mean, realise, recognise, suppose, doubt, agree, disagree, imagine (= suppose)

Feelings and wishes

like, love, hate, dislike, prefer, adore, want, wish, need, mind, appreciate, care

Senses and appearance

see, hear, smell, taste, feel, seem, look (= seem), appear (= seem), sound, recognise

Possession

have (= own), own, possess, belong, include, contain, consist of, lack

Being and measuring

be, cost, weigh, measure, fit, depend, deserve, matter, resemble, involve, equal

Exceptions: when a state verb becomes an action

Some of these verbs have a second meaning that is a real action or process. In that meaning, the continuous form is correct.

In informal English you may also hear love or like in the continuous to show enjoyment at the moment: I am loving this exhibition! It is fine in speech, but avoid it in exams and formal writing.
