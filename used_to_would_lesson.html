Past repeated actions and states

B1 grammar lesson

I used to walk to school. I would walk to school. I walked to school.

Three ways to talk about the past. In these sentences they mean the same thing. In other sentences, only one or two of them are correct. Let's find out when and why.

Three forms, three jobs

used to + verb

Something happened again and again, or was true, in the past. It is not true now.

  • Habits (repeated actions): I used to play football every weekend.
  • States (situations, feelings): I used to have long hair. There used to be a cinema here.
  • Negative: I didn't use to like tea. (no -d!)
  • Question: Did you use to live here?
  • Only for the past. For habits now, say usually: I usually get up at 7.

would + verb

An action we did again and again in the past. It sounds like a memory or a story.

  • Only habits: Every summer we would go to the sea.
  • Never states: I would have long hair.
  • Usually we first say when: When I was a kid, I would…
  • Short form: We'd swim every day.
  • It doesn't have a special negative or question form for habits. Use didn't use to or the past simple.

past simple (went, had, played)

The most general past form. It works almost everywhere.

  • One action: I broke my arm in 2015.
  • Habits and states too: I played football every weekend. I had long hair.
  • How many times: I visited Paris three times.
  • How long: I lived there for five years.
  • It doesn't show if things are different now. Used to shows the change.

How are they similar?

All three talk about the past.

All three can describe a past habit. For habits, you can change one for another and the meaning stays the same.

The past simple can almost always replace used to and would.

Used to and would are the same only for repeated actions.

The difference in three lines

used to habits and states that are not true now.

would habits only, often in stories and memories.

past simple everything, including one-time actions, numbers of times and periods of time.

Tip: how people tell stories

People often start with used to (to set the scene) and then continue with would:

My grandma used to live in a small village. Every morning she would feed the chickens, and then she'd make breakfast for everyone.

State verbs: never with would

State verbs describe feelings, thoughts, owning and being. They are not actions, so we can't "repeat" them. With state verbs, use used to or the past simple. Never would.

GroupVerbsExample
Feelingslike, love, hate, prefer, wantI used to hate vegetables.
Thinkingknow, believe, understand, remember, think (= have an opinion)I used to believe in Santa Claus.
Having and beingbe, have (= own), own, belong, seemWe used to have a big dog.

Right and wrong

  • ✓He used to be very shy.
  • ✓He was very shy.
  • ✗He would be very shy.
  • ✓She used to love chocolate.
  • ✗She would love chocolate. (This means she wants it now!)

Watch out: some verbs have two meanings

have: We used to have a car. (own, a state, no would)
We would have lunch at two o'clock. (eat, an action, would is OK)

think: I used to think maths was boring. (opinion, no would)
At night I would think about my future. (an activity in your head, would is OK)

live: a long situation, so treat it like a state.
I used to live in Odesa. ✓ I would live in Odesa.

Don't confuse

I used to work at night. = I did it in the past. I don't do it now.

I'm used to working at night. = It's normal for me now. It's not strange or difficult. (be used to + -ing)
