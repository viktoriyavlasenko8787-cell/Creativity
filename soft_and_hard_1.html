Hard & Soft Skills in IT

English grammar practice · advanced (C1)

Hard and soft skills in IT

Practise Conditionals 0, 1, 2 and 3 while discussing what really makes a great IT professional: sharp technical skills, or the ability to work well with people.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • use Conditionals 0, 1, 2 and 3 correctly, with the right structure and time reference;
  • recognise and fix common mistakes in conditional sentences;
  • use a wide range of introductory words beyond if;
  • discuss hard and soft skills in IT using natural conditional sentences.

  4. Have you ever seen a project fail because of poor communication rather than a technical problem? What happened?
  5. Should soft skills be taught in computer science courses? Why or why not?

Grammar: Conditionals 0, 1, 2 and 3

In every example, the structure is shown in bold, and the predicate — the verb group of each clause — is highlighted in red in both the if-clause and the result clause.

All four at a glance

Real or unreal? Conditionals 0 and 1 describe real situations (facts, or things that can really happen). Conditionals 2 and 3 describe unreal or imagined situations — 2 in the present/future, 3 in the past.

Task 10. Describe and imagine

Look at the picture of a software team in a stand-up meeting.

Describe and imagine

  1. Describe what you see: who is doing what, and how do people seem to feel?
  2. Conditional 0: what generally happens when a build fails like this?
  3. Conditional 1: what will the team do next if they can't fix the bug today?
  4. Conditional 2: imagine you were the stressed developer. What would you do differently?
  5. Conditional 3: what might have caused this? What would have happened if someone had tested the code earlier?

Now answer this question

"If your team had one extra soft skill and one extra hard skill for free, which two would you choose — and what would change?"

Answer using at least one Conditional 2 sentence and one Conditional 3 sentence.
