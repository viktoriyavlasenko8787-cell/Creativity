Level 1: The rules

Both tenses link the past to now. Simple focuses on the result. Continuous focuses on the activity. In every example, the predicate is red.

Present perfect simple

subject + have/has + past participle (V3)

  • A result or finished action. I've finished the campaign.
  • How many / how much. She's won five matches today.
  • Life experience. Have you ever played a horror game?
  • State verbs. I've had this console for years.
+I/you/we/they have ('ve) played
he/she/it has ('s) played
−I haven't played / she hasn't played
?Have you played? / Has she played?
Yes, I have. / No, she hasn't.

Present perfect continuous

subject + have/has + been + verb-ing

  • The activity itself. I've been playing all afternoon.
  • How long. How long have you been streaming?
  • Unfinished or temporary. He's been building a castle, but it isn't ready.
  • Visible side effects. Your eyes are red. Have you been gaming all night?
+I/you/we/they have been playing
he/she/it has been playing
−I haven't been playing / she hasn't been playing
?Have you been playing? / Has she been playing?
Yes, I have. / No, she hasn't.

Action verbs and state verbs

The present perfect continuous is used only with action verbs. With a state verb, use the present perfect simple, even with for, since and how long.

Action verbs

Describe something we do. The action can start, stop and be in progress.

play, build, stream, practise, download, wait, run, talk

✓ Continuous is possible:
I've been playing for two hours.

State verbs

Describe a state: what we think, feel, own or sense, or what something is. Nothing is in progress.

know, like, want, have (own), believe, seem, be

✗ No continuous:
I've known him for years. (not I've been knowing)

Quick test. Can you say "I'm in the middle of …ing"? I'm in the middle of playing works, so play is an action verb. I'm in the middle of knowing is impossible, so know is a state verb.

State verbs by group

GroupVerbs✗ Don't say✓ Say
Thinking and knowingknow, believe, understand, remember, realise, meanI've been knowing him for years.I've known him for years.
Feelings and wantslike, love, hate, want, prefer, needShe's been wanting this game since May.She's wanted this game since May.
Possessionhave (own), own, belong, possessI've been owning this PC since 2022.I've owned this PC since 2022.
Senses and appearancesee, hear, smell, taste, seem, look (= seem)The boss has been seeming easy so far.The boss has seemed easy so far.
Being and containingbe, exist, consist, contain, includeThe bundle has been including three games.The bundle has included three games.
