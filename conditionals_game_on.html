The rules

Four conditionals at a glance

TypeStructure and exampleWhat it's forTime markersTypical linkers
Zero If / When + present simple, present simpleIf your health bar drops to zero, your character respawns at the last checkpoint. Facts, rules of a game or system, things that are always true, habitual results. always, usually, every time, whenever, in general if, when, whenever, every time, unless
First If + present simple, will / can / may / might + infinitive (or an imperative)If we finish this quest tonight, we'll unlock the final boss. Real, likely situations in the present or future and their probable results. Promises, warnings, plans. tonight, tomorrow, soon, next week, this weekend, by Friday unless, as long as, provided / providing (that), as soon as, in case, otherwise; inversion: Should you…
Second If + past simple / past continuous, would / could / might + infinitiveIf I had more free time, I would finally finish that open-world RPG. Imaginary or unlikely situations now or in the future. Advice (If I were you…), dreams, polite distance. now, today, these days, at the moment, one day (imaginary) if, unless, even if, suppose / supposing, imagine, what if; inversion: Were I to…
Third If + past perfect, would / could / might + have + past participleIf I had saved the game, I wouldn't have lost three hours of progress. Imaginary past: things that didn't happen and their imaginary results. Regrets, criticism, relief. yesterday, last night, last season, back then, in 2019, at launch if, if only, but for, even if; inversion: Had I known…

C1 upgrades

  • Inversion (formal, no if): Should you need help… First Were the studio to cancel it… Second Had I known… Third
  • Unless = if not: Unless the patch fixes it, players will leave.
  • Provided that / as long as / on condition that make the condition stronger.
  • If only + past perfect for strong regret: If only I'd pre-ordered it!

Watch out

  • No will or would after if: If it will rain rains… If I would have had known…
  • If I were you is standard in formal English; was is common in speech.
  • Use a comma when the if-clause comes first; no comma when it comes second.
  • Zero vs first: zero is always true; first is about one specific future situation.
